Textual Certainty Guidelines

Use these criteria to evaluate variants and assign a rating (A–D).

1. External Evidence (The Manuscripts)

  • Date & Age: Earlier witnesses generally carry more weight, as they are closer to the autograph.
  • Geographical Distribution: A reading supported by manuscripts from diverse regions (e.g., Alexandrian, Western, Byzantine) is usually stronger than one isolated to a single geographic area.
  • Text-Type Reliability: Alexandrian manuscripts (e.g., Sinaiticus, Vaticanus, P75) are traditionally considered highly reliable, but must be weighed holistically against other families.

2. Internal Evidence (Scribal Habits)

  • Shorter Reading (Lectio Brevior): Scribes were far more likely to add explanatory text (glosses) than to intentionally delete original text.
  • Harder Reading (Lectio Difficilior): Scribes often "smoothed out" difficult grammar, apparent contradictions, or harsh theological statements. The rougher reading is often original.
  • Harmonization: Scribes tended to alter passages to match parallel accounts, a common occurrence in the Synoptic Gospels.

UBS Ratings Scale

  • A: Virtually certain (strong external and internal support).
  • B: Highly probable, but some doubt remains.
  • C: Difficult to decide; evidence is evenly split.
  • D: Very uncertain; the original text is highly debatable.
